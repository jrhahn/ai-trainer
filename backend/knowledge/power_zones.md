# Cycling Power Training Zones

## Overview

Power-based training zones provide an objective framework for prescribing and
monitoring exercise intensity in cyclists. Unlike heart-rate zones, power is
instantaneous and unaffected by heat, fatigue, or caffeine, making it the gold
standard for intensity control.

## The 7-Zone Model (Coggan)

The most widely used framework divides training intensity into seven zones based
on a percentage of Functional Threshold Power (FTP):

| Zone | Name | % FTP | Duration | Physiological Target |
|------|------|--------|----------|----------------------|
| Z1 | Active Recovery | < 55% | 30–90 min | Blood flow, glycogen restoration |
| Z2 | Endurance | 56–75% | 1–6 h | Aerobic base, fat oxidation |
| Z3 | Tempo | 76–90% | 20–60 min | Lactate clearance, aerobic power |
| Z4 | Lactate Threshold | 91–105% | 10–60 min | Raise FTP, improve buffering |
| Z5 | VO2max | 106–120% | 3–8 min | Maximal aerobic capacity (VO2max) |
| Z6 | Anaerobic Capacity | 121–150% | 30 s–3 min | Anaerobic energy systems |
| Z7 | Neuromuscular | > 150% | < 30 s | Maximal sprint power |

## Functional Threshold Power (FTP)

FTP is defined as the highest average power a rider can sustain for approximately
60 minutes. It is the cornerstone of power-based training:

- **Field test**: 95% of best 20-minute power (the 5% discount accounts for the
  shorter test duration vs. a true 60-min effort)
- **Ramp test**: 75% of peak 1-minute power achieved in a step-ramp protocol
- **Lactate testing**: power at 4 mmol/L blood lactate (gold standard but
  requires laboratory access)

FTP typically improves 5–15% per structured training block for well-motivated
amateur cyclists. Elite cyclists may see smaller relative gains because they start
from a higher baseline.

## Training Distribution and Zone Usage

Research on optimal training intensity distribution for endurance athletes
consistently points to a polarised or pyramidal approach:

- **80/20 rule**: approximately 80% of training time in Z1–Z2 (below lactate
  threshold) and 20% in Z4–Z5 (at or above threshold)
- **Sweet-spot training** (88–95% FTP, upper Z3/lower Z4) is a time-efficient
  alternative for time-constrained athletes; it provides a strong training
  stimulus with manageable fatigue

## Practical Zone Application

**Zone 2 (Endurance)**: The foundation of all endurance training. Long Z2 rides
build mitochondrial density, enhance fat oxidation, and develop the aerobic
enzyme systems. Aim for 60–80% of weekly training volume in Z2.

**Zone 4 (Threshold)**: Classic FTP work such as 2×20 min or 3×15 min at
95–105% FTP. This is the primary driver of FTP improvement. Limit to 2 sessions
per week maximum; each session requires 24–48 hours of recovery.

**Zone 5 (VO2max)**: Short maximal intervals (4×4 min, 5×3 min) at 110–120%
FTP. Highly effective for raising VO2max. High neuromuscular cost — 1–2 sessions
per week maximum, not back-to-back.

## Power Measurement

Consumer-grade power meters (crank-based, pedal-based, or hub-based) typically
have ±1–2% accuracy, which is sufficient for zone-based training. Consistency of
calibration matters more than absolute accuracy when tracking progress over time.
