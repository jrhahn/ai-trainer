# Sweet-Spot Training for Cyclists

## Definition

Sweet-spot training (SST) targets the power range between upper Tempo and lower
Threshold: approximately **88–95% of FTP** in the Coggan 7-zone model, or roughly
Zones 3–4. The term was popularised by Frank Overton (FasCat Coaching) to describe
intensity that provides a high training stimulus per unit time with manageable
fatigue.

## Physiological Basis

At sweet-spot intensity, cyclists are working near but below their lactate
threshold:

- **High mitochondrial stress**: sufficient to drive mitochondrial biogenesis and
  increase mitochondrial density
- **Sustainable aerobic demand**: lactate accumulates modestly but clearance keeps
  pace with production for 20–60 minute blocks
- **Glycolytic contribution**: meaningful glycolytic component improves muscle
  buffering capacity and carbohydrate oxidation efficiency
- **Cardiovascular load**: cardiac output is elevated, providing a sustained
  training stimulus for stroke volume development

## Key Adaptations from Sweet-Spot Training

1. **Raised FTP**: Consistent SST work is one of the most effective methods for
   improving FTP in amateur cyclists. Studies on trained cyclists show FTP gains
   of 5–10% in 6–12 week blocks.

2. **Improved lactate threshold**: Sweet-spot work upregulates lactate
   transporters (MCT1, MCT4) and oxidative enzymes, raising the power at which
   lactate begins to accumulate.

3. **Increased time-to-fatigue at threshold**: By habituating to riding at the
   upper aerobic range, athletes extend the duration they can sustain near-FTP
   power.

## Classic Sweet-Spot Workouts

**2×20 min at 88–93% FTP** (10 min recovery between efforts):
- A staple SST workout; total of 40 min in the sweet spot
- Good for developing sustained sub-threshold power
- Appropriate for intermediate cyclists; 2–3 sessions/week maximum

**3×15 min at 90–95% FTP** (8 min recovery):
- Slightly higher intensity; good for moving FTP ceiling
- Recommended for cyclists with an existing aerobic base

**1×60 min at 85–90% FTP**:
- A single long SST effort; psychologically demanding
- Excellent for building mental toughness and fat oxidation at moderate-high power
- Common in Gran Fondo and century-ride preparation

## Sweet-Spot vs. Polarized Training: Which to Choose?

| Factor | Sweet-Spot | Polarized |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Training hours/week | 6–12 | ≥ 8–12 |
| Primary goal | FTP improvement | VO2max and general fitness |
| Recovery demand | Moderate–high | Low–moderate |
| Time to adaptation | 4–8 weeks | 6–12 weeks |
| Best for | Time-limited athletes | High-volume athletes |

Most coaches recommend a periodised approach: use **sweet-spot work in the base
and build phases** to efficiently raise FTP, then shift toward **more polarized
distribution** in the competition phase where preserving freshness and developing
peak speed matter more.

## Fatigue Considerations

Sweet-spot training accumulates fatigue faster than pure aerobic (Z2) work.
Practical guidelines:

- Limit sweet-spot blocks to 2–3 sessions per week
- Always follow a hard SST day with an easy Z1/Z2 day
- Include one recovery week (reduced volume and intensity) every 3–4 weeks
- Monitor chronic training load (CTL) and avoid increasing it by more than
  5–7 TSS/day per week

## Recommended Progression

1. **Weeks 1–2**: 2×15 min at 88% FTP
2. **Weeks 3–4**: 2×20 min at 90% FTP
3. **Weeks 5–6**: 3×20 min at 90% FTP
4. **Week 7**: Recovery week (volume –40%, intensity –10%)
5. **Week 8**: FTP test; adjust zones
