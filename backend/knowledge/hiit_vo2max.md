# High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT) and VO2max Development

## Overview

High-intensity interval training (HIIT) is characterized by repeated bouts of exercise at intensities ≥90% of peak heart rate (HRpeak), interspersed with recovery periods. It is one of the most potent stimuli for improving VO2max, time to exhaustion (TTE), and lactate threshold performance — particularly in already well-trained endurance athletes where high-volume low-intensity training alone produces diminishing returns.

## Key Evidence: Comparing Training Modalities

A landmark randomized controlled trial (Stöggl & Sperlich, 2014, Frontiers in Physiology, CC BY 4.0) compared four training strategies over 9 weeks in 48 well-trained endurance athletes (VO2peak 62.6 ± 7.1 mL/min/kg):

| Group | Intensity Distribution | VO2peak Change | TTE Change | V/Ppeak Change |
|-------|----------------------|---------------|------------|----------------|
| Polarized (POL) | 68% LOW / 6% threshold / 26% HIGH | **+11.7%** *** | **+17.4%** *** | **+5.1%** ** |
| HIIT | 43% LOW / 0% threshold / 57% HIGH | +4.8% * | +8.8% ** | +4.4% ** |
| Threshold (THR) | 46% LOW / 54% threshold / 0% HIGH | no change | no change | no change |
| High Volume (HVT) | 83% LOW / 16% threshold / 1% HIGH | no change | no change | no change |

*p<0.05, **p<0.01, ***p<0.001 within groups pre vs. post

**Key finding**: In well-trained athletes (VO2peak > 52 mL/min/kg), exclusive HIIT yields only +4.8% VO2peak — significantly less than polarized training's +11.7%. Pure threshold or pure high-volume training produces no further improvements in already well-trained athletes.

## HIIT Protocol Details

### Standard 4×4 Protocol (Helgerud et al., 2007)
The most validated HIIT protocol for VO2max development:
- 4 intervals × 4 minutes at 90–95% HRpeak
- 3 minutes active recovery between intervals at ~70% HRpeak
- 20-minute warm-up at ~75% HRpeak
- 15-minute cool-down
- Frequency: 2–3× per week produces ~0.5% VO2max increase per session in moderately trained athletes

### Condensed HIIT Blocks
- 10–13 sessions over 2 weeks: +7% VO2peak (Stølen et al., 2005)
- Effect diminishes with repeated blocks without adequate low-intensity base
- 9 weeks of 27 HIIT sessions: only +4.8% VO2peak vs. 14 HIIT sessions within POL: +11.7%

## Physiological Mechanisms

HIIT drives VO2max improvements through:

1. **Central adaptations**: Increased stroke volume and cardiac output (Helgerud et al., 2007)
2. **Blood volume expansion**: Increased plasma volume buffers lactate and delivers O2 more effectively
3. **Peripheral adaptations**:
   - Enhanced O2 extraction in working muscles
   - Mitochondrial biogenesis and increased oxidative capacity (Daussin et al., 2007, 2008)
   - Improved aerobic AND anaerobic metabolism
4. **Lactate threshold shift**: V/P at 4 mmol/L blood lactate increases significantly after HIIT (+5.6%) and POL (+8.1%)

## Training Intensity Zones for HIIT

Three-zone intensity model:
- **LOW (Zone 1)**: <2 mmol/L blood lactate / <80% HRpeak — aerobic base, fat oxidation, mitochondrial density
- **THRESHOLD (Zone 2)**: 3–5 mmol/L blood lactate — lactate threshold training
- **HIGH (Zone 3)**: >90% HRpeak / >6 mmol/L lactate — VO2max stimulation, anaerobic capacity

## Practical Application for Coaches

### Who benefits most from HIIT?
- **Untrained to moderately trained athletes**: Strong VO2max gains from HIIT alone (+9–11% over 6–10 weeks)
- **Well-trained athletes (VO2peak > 55 mL/min/kg)**: Need HIIT as part of polarized distribution, not in isolation
- **Athletes plateaued on HVT**: Adding 2× HIIT sessions/week breaks adaptation stagnation

### Warning Signs of Excessive HIIT
- Body mass reduction (Stöggl & Sperlich found 3.7% BM loss with exclusive HIIT — undesirable in endurance athletes)
- Diminishing returns after first 4–6 weeks of a HIIT block
- Risk of overreaching if 12+ HIIT sessions packed into 16 days without periodized recovery
- Greater VO2peak gains per session (0.84%/session) achieved with polarized training vs. exclusive HIIT (0.18%/session)

### Optimal HIIT Structure Within Annual Plan
1. **Foundation phase**: Establish aerobic base (80–85% low intensity)
2. **Build phase**: Introduce 1–2 HIIT sessions/week alongside long low-intensity rides
3. **Intensity block**: 2–3 weeks of increased HIIT (2–3× per week) followed by 1 week recovery
4. **Pre-competition**: Taper HIIT volume, maintain intensity

## Session Recommendations

### HIIT for Cyclists
- **Classic VO2max intervals**: 5×3 min at 105–120% FTP (functional threshold power), 3 min rest
- **4×4 protocol**: 4×4 min at ~120% FTP, 4 min easy recovery
- **30/15s intervals (Billat)**: 30 s at 130% FTP, 15 s recovery — good for athletes struggling with pacing
- **2×20 min at threshold** (not true HIIT, but lactate threshold): 95–100% FTP

### Frequency
- 2× per week in build phase
- Maximum 3× per week in peak intensity block (short blocks only: 2–3 weeks)
- Minimum 1× per week in maintenance

## Key Metrics to Track

- **VO2max/VO2peak** (gold standard — lab test)
- **Power at 4 mmol/L lactate (V/P4)**: Best field predictor of time trial performance
- **Peak power output (V/Ppeak)**: 5.1% increase from polarized, 4.4% from HIIT in 9 weeks
- **Time to exhaustion (TTE)**: 17.4% improvement with polarized (includes HIIT component), 8.8% with pure HIIT
