# Tapering and Peaking for Endurance Events

## What Is Tapering?

Tapering is the systematic reduction of training load in the days to weeks before a target event (A-race). Its purpose is to dissipate accumulated fatigue while preserving or enhancing the fitness adaptations built during the training block. Done correctly, tapering produces performance gains of **2–8%** above the pre-taper baseline.

The fundamental insight: **fitness is largely maintained during a taper, while fatigue decreases rapidly**. The result is a net performance increase.

## The Science: Why Tapering Works

### Physiological Adaptations During Taper
- **Glycogen resynthesis**: Muscle glycogen stores increase 20–30% above baseline (even without carb-loading) when training load drops
- **Neuromuscular recovery**: Motor unit recruitment patterns normalize; explosive force production improves
- **Blood volume**: Red blood cell mass increases slightly; oxygen-carrying capacity improves
- **Hormonal restoration**: Testosterone:cortisol ratio normalizes (key marker of recovery from overtraining)
- **Muscle repair**: Residual microdamage from heavy training resolves; contractile force returns
- **Immune function**: Suppressed immune function during heavy training recovers (reduces illness risk)

### The Training Impulse Model (TRIMP / PMC)
In Performance Management Chart (PMC) terms:
- **CTL (Chronic Training Load)**: Your "fitness" — 42-day exponential rolling average
- **ATL (Acute Training Load)**: Your "fatigue" — 7-day exponential rolling average
- **TSB/Form = CTL − ATL**: Positive = fresh; negative = fatigued

**Optimal race-day TSB**: +5 to +20 for most cyclists; up to +25 for ultra-endurance events

## Taper Duration and Structure

### Standard Taper Lengths

| Event Type | Taper Duration | Peak TSB Target |
|-----------|----------------|-----------------|
| 1-hour time trial / criterium | 5–7 days | +10 to +20 |
| Gran Fondo / Century (5–7h) | 10–14 days | +5 to +15 |
| Stage race (multi-day) | 7–10 days | +5 to +10 (don't over-taper) |
| Ironman / ultra marathon | 14–21 days | +10 to +25 |
| Marathon | 14–21 days | +10 to +20 |

### Exponential Taper (Most Evidence-Based)
Reduce training load gradually, not abruptly:
- Week -3 before race: Normal training or slight reduction
- Week -2 before race: **~50% volume reduction** (maintain intensity)
- Week -1 before race: **~70% volume reduction** (maintain intensity)
- Race week: Short openers only; race day

### Volume vs. Intensity: The Critical Rule
**Reduce volume. Maintain intensity.**

The most common tapering mistake is reducing both volume AND intensity. Research consistently shows:
- Maintaining intensity during taper preserves VO2max, lactate threshold adaptations
- Reducing intensity causes neuromuscular detraining within 10–14 days
- Volume can be reduced 40–70% without losing fitness

## Week-by-Week Structure

### 2 Weeks Out (14–8 Days Before Race)
- **Volume**: Reduce to ~60% of peak week
- **Intensity**: Keep threshold and VO2max sessions but shorten them
  - Example: 2×20min threshold → 1×20min or 2×12min
- **Long ride**: Reduce from 5h → 3h; maintain effort in final 30–45 min
- **Frequency**: Same number of rides (don't eliminate sessions)

### Race Week (7–3 Days Before Race)
- **Volume**: 30–40% of peak week
- **Key session (4–5 days out)**: "Pre-race sharpener" — one session with race-intensity efforts
  - Example: 2×8min at race power/pace, fully recovered
  - Purpose: Prime neuromuscular system without adding fatigue
- **Days 3–1**: Short, easy rides (30–45 min) with 3–5 × 1-min "openers" at high intensity
- **Day before**: Rest or 20–30 min very easy with 3 quick accelerations

### Openers (1–2 Days Before Race)
- 20–40 minute easy ride
- Include 5–8 × 30-second bursts at 120–130% FTP (full recovery between)
- Purpose: Activate fast-twitch fibers, prime lactate clearance, stimulate neural pathways
- **Do NOT leave legs feeling tired** — if they do, it was too much

## Carbohydrate Loading

### When to Use It
- Events lasting > 90 minutes at sustained effort
- Unnecessary for races under 75–90 minutes

### Standard Protocol (3-Day Carb Load)
- Day -3 to -1 before race: Increase carbohydrate to **8–12 g/kg body weight/day**
- Concurrently reduce fiber and fat intake (less bulk, avoids GI issues on race day)
- Maintain protein at ~1.6g/kg

**Practical example (75kg athlete)**:
- Target: 600–900g carbohydrate/day
- Sources: Rice, pasta, bread, potatoes, sports drinks, bananas, energy bars
- Spread across 4–6 meals

### What Carb Loading Does NOT Do
- It does NOT increase VO2max
- It does NOT "supercharge" sprint performance
- It DOES increase endurance capacity beyond 90 minutes by ~2–3%
- It WILL cause temporary weight gain (~1.5–2kg from stored glycogen + water — expected and fine)

## Pre-Race Day and Race Morning

### Day Before
- **Training**: 20–30 min easy with openers (see above)
- **Nutrition**: Last carb-load meal, finish by 8pm
- **Hydration**: Well-hydrated; urine pale yellow
- **Sleep**: Priority — aim for ≥8 hours
- **Preparation**: Lay out gear, charge devices, check weather

### Race Morning (3–4 Hours Before Start)
- **Breakfast**: 1–4 g/kg carbohydrates; familiar, gut-tested foods
  - Options: Oatmeal, rice, banana, white toast with jam, sports drink
  - Avoid: High fiber, high fat, anything untested in training
- **Hydration**: 500–750ml water or electrolyte drink
- **Caffeine**: 3–6 mg/kg body weight, 60 min before start
  - 75kg athlete: 225–450mg caffeine (2–4 espressos or pre-workout supplement)

### Warm-Up
- For events < 30 minutes: **Warm-up is critical** — 20–30 min with progressive efforts
- For events > 2 hours: 5–10 min easy warm-up is sufficient; the first 20 min of race IS the warm-up
- Include race-pace efforts in final 5–8 min of warm-up

## The Mental/Psychological Taper

### Taper Tantrums (Very Common)
During the taper, most athletes experience:
- Legs feeling heavy or "dead" (actually normal — body recalibrating from reduced training)
- Anxiety, irritability, low motivation (dopamine/serotonin adjustment to lower training volume)
- Phantom aches and hypochondria (common)
- Feeling "unfit" despite being fitter than ever

**All of these are normal and temporary.** Trust the process. The flat/heavy feeling during the taper does NOT predict race performance.

### Evidence for Pre-Race Confidence
- Athletes who believe they are "on form" perform better than equally-fit athletes who doubt their form
- Pre-race routine consistency reduces anxiety
- Previous positive results in similar conditions are the best confidence booster

## Signs of a Well-Executed Taper

On race day, a successful taper typically feels like:
- Legs feel "springy" and reactive
- Easy efforts feel surprisingly fast
- Heart rate responds readily to effort changes
- Motivation is high
- Sleep was good in the final days

## Common Tapering Mistakes

| Mistake | Effect | Fix |
|---------|--------|-----|
| Cutting intensity along with volume | Lose fitness, feel flat | Keep race-pace efforts in taper |
| Starting taper too late | Carry fatigue into race | Begin 10–14 days out for major events |
| Over-tapering (too much reduction) | Lose "race sharpness" | Maintain short race-pace efforts weekly |
| Skipping openers | Sluggish in race's first hour | Include openers 1–2 days before |
| Eating unfamiliar foods race week | GI distress | Stick to gut-tested foods |
| New gear on race day | Mechanical issues, blisters | Test everything in training |

## Peaking for Multiple Races in a Season

### A/B/C Race Hierarchy
- **A races** (1–3/year): Full taper + carb load; extended recovery after
- **B races** (3–6/year): 5–7 day mini-taper; no full carb load; reduced recovery
- **C races** (as many as desired): Training race; no taper; use as benchmark or training stimulus

### Recovery After A-Race
Allow full recovery before resuming base training:
- After a 1-2h event: 3–5 days easy
- After a century/sportive (5–7h): 7–10 days easy
- After Ironman: **3–4 weeks** reduced training before resuming structure
